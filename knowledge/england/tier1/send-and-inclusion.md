# SEND and inclusion

Governed by the SEND Code of Practice: 0 to 25 years, under the Children and Families Act 2014.

## Four areas of need

Communication and interaction; cognition and learning; social, emotional and mental health;
sensory and/or physical needs.

## The graduated approach

**Assess, plan, do, review** — a cycle, revisited with increasing detail. Most needs are met through
**SEN Support** in school. Where they cannot be, the local authority may carry out an education,
health and care needs assessment, which can lead to an **EHCP**: a legal document setting out
provision the authority must secure, reviewed at least annually.

Every school has a **SENCo**, a qualified teacher responsible for coordinating provision. Class and
subject teachers remain responsible for the progress of every pupil they teach, including those
supported by teaching assistants — this is stated explicitly in the Code.

## Quality first teaching

The first response to a learning need is teaching adapted for that pupil, not separate provision.
"Differentiation" has largely given way to "adaptive teaching" in current usage.

## Exam access arrangements

Arrangements such as extra time, a reader, a scribe, a word processor, supervised rest breaks or a
separate room must reflect the pupil's **normal way of working** and be supported by evidence of
need. They are applied for through JCQ processes by the exams officer, usually with an assessment by
a suitably qualified assessor. They are not awarded on request, and they are not a reward.

## Equality Act 2010

Schools must make reasonable adjustments for disabled pupils and must not discriminate on any
protected characteristic. This duty is anticipatory: it applies before a particular pupil arrives.
