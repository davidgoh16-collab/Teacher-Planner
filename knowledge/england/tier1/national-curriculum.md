# The national curriculum in England

Applies to maintained schools. Academies and free schools are not bound by it but must offer a
broad and balanced curriculum, and in practice most follow it closely at Key Stages 1–3.

## Core and foundation subjects

**Core** at all of Key Stages 1–4: English, mathematics, science.

**Foundation**, varying by key stage: art and design, citizenship (KS3–4), computing, design and
technology, languages (KS2–3), geography, history, music, physical education.

## By key stage

- **KS1–2** — English, maths, science, art and design, computing, design and technology, geography,
  history, music, PE; languages at KS2.
- **KS3** — the above plus citizenship; art, design and technology, and music remain compulsory.
- **KS4** — core subjects plus computing, citizenship, PE. Schools must also offer at least one
  subject from each of the arts, design and technology, humanities and modern foreign languages.

## Also compulsory, outside the national curriculum

- **Religious education** at every key stage, following the locally agreed syllabus. Parents may
  withdraw their child.
- **Relationships education** (primary), **relationships and sex education** (secondary) and
  **health education**. Parents may request withdrawal from sex education only.
- A daily act of **collective worship**.
- **Careers guidance** from Year 7 to 13.

## The English Baccalaureate

Not a qualification — a performance measure covering GCSE English, maths, sciences, a language, and
either history or geography.

## Coming, but not yet in force

The Curriculum and Assessment Review reported in November 2025, recommending a reduction in KS4 exam
volume, an oracy framework, and broader compulsory citizenship. First teaching is targeted for 2028.
Do not present any of it as current practice, but do not contradict it when discussing the longer
term.
