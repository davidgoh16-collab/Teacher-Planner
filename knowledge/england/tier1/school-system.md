# How schooling is organised in England

## Key stages and year groups

| Key stage | Years | Ages |
|---|---|---|
| EYFS | Nursery, Reception | 3–5 |
| Key Stage 1 | Years 1–2 | 5–7 |
| Key Stage 2 | Years 3–6 | 7–11 |
| Key Stage 3 | Years 7–9 | 11–14 |
| Key Stage 4 | Years 10–11 | 14–16 |
| Key Stage 5 / sixth form | Years 12–13 | 16–18 |

Statutory assessment points: the EYFS Profile at the end of Reception, the phonics screening check
in Year 1, national curriculum tests ("SATs") at the end of Key Stage 2, GCSEs at the end of Key
Stage 4. Key Stage 3 has no national tests.

Education or training is compulsory until 18, though it need not be at school after 16.

## Types of school

Maintained schools (community, foundation, voluntary aided/controlled) follow the national
curriculum and are overseen by the local authority. Academies and free schools are funded directly
and need not follow the national curriculum, though they must teach a broad and balanced curriculum
and follow the statutory guidance that applies to all schools. Many belong to a multi-academy trust.
Independent schools charge fees and set their own curriculum. Special schools and alternative
provision serve pupils whose needs cannot be met in a mainstream setting.

Every school has a **URN** (Unique Reference Number) in Get Information About Schools, the DfE's
public register. It is the reliable way to identify a school.

## The school year

Three terms, each usually split by a half-term break: autumn, spring and summer. The year normally
runs early September to late July, about 190 teaching days plus five INSET days.

**Term dates are set locally** by the local authority, academy trust or school. There is no national
list. Never state a school's term dates unless the teacher has given them.
